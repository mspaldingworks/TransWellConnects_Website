Transgender Spiritual Trauma and Reconciliation

For too many transgender people, faith communities have been a source of harm instead of home. This November, Transpire and All People’s Justice Center are bringing clergy and lay leaders from across Louisville into one room to do something they don’t do often enough: stop talking and listen.

We’re looking for transgender speakers willing to share their experience with church, faith, and spiritual community, on their own terms. Your voice will help congregations understand the real cost of spiritual harm and what it would actually take to become places of welcome, repair, and solidarity.

Who we’re looking for

Transgender, nonbinary, and gender-expansive people whose lives have been shaped by a faith community, whether you:

01

Left

Walked away from a church or faith community because of the spiritual trauma you experienced there.

02

Found reconciliation

Experienced spiritual harm and later found your way to healing, or to a church or faith community that truly welcomed you.

03

Are still searching

Are still looking for a spiritual home, or still deciding whether you want one at all.

Every faith background is welcome, including none. You don’t need a polished story, and your story doesn’t need a happy ending. There is no right answer here, only your truth.

How we’ll care for our speakers

Telling this kind of story takes something out of a person. We’ve built this event so you don’t carry it alone.

  • You’ll be paid.Your time, story, and expertise are worth compensating.
  • A pre-talk before the event.We’ll meet with you ahead of time to hear your goals for the session and answer your questions.
  • No surprise questions.Questions are prepared in advance, and you’ll see them before the night of.
  • You set the limits.Tell us what’s off the table, and you can pass on anything, no explanation needed.
  • A moderator has your back.They field any unintentionally harmful or off-topic questions so you never have to.
  • Prepared listeners.Clergy and lay leaders receive guidelines on how to listen well before they walk in the door.
  • Support afterward.Support people will be available after the event for the trans folks who take part.

Why we’re doing this

We’re being explicit with everyone in the room, speakers and congregations alike, about what this session is for:

  1. To hear what trans people need from faith communities for those communities to become genuinely welcoming and affirming.
  2. To learn what the trans community needs and wants from congregations that say they stand in solidarity.
  3. To move congregations from good intentions to concrete change, and to hold them accountable for it.
